# Doc C — Clinical Trial Protocol Content

## B.1 General information

B.1.1 Cover/title page: protocol title, unique protocol identifying number, and date; amendments carry an amendment number and date.

B.1.2 Name and address of the sponsor.

B.1.3 Name and title of the person(s) authorised to sign the protocol and its amendments for the sponsor.

B.1.4 Table of contents; list of abbreviations/terminology (in alphabetical order).

B.1.5 Protocol Summary / Synopsis / Abstract — a standalone summary of the study covering the background, objective/aim, population, intervention, methods, timeframe, outcome measures, and proposed analysis, in plain language with acronyms defined at first use. For multi-site studies, indicates the scope of activities occurring at each site (e.g., recruitment/consent only, performing study procedures, or data/sample handling only) (UHN REB CAPCR Presubmission Toolkit).

---

## B.2 Background information

B.2.1 Introduction:
- Name and description of the investigational product(s).
- A summary of findings from non-clinical *in vitro* or *in vivo* studies that potentially have clinical significance and from clinical trials that are relevant to the trial.
- Summary of the known and potential risks and benefits, if any, to human participants.
- Description of and justification for the route of administration, dosage, dosage regimen and treatment period(s), together with a brief description of the drug/device, its mechanism of action, and whether it is currently in use and approved.
- A statement that the trial will be conducted in compliance with the protocol, Good Clinical Practice (GCP) and the applicable regulatory requirement(s).
- Description of the population to be studied.
- References to literature and data relevant to the trial, including a focused literature review of relevant findings and the gap in the literature that the trial addresses.

B.2.2 Rationale: The reasons for conducting the research in light of current knowledge, including the need/problem that is the basis of the project, its cause, and possible solutions.

B.2.3 Impact: How the study will add to science, change practice, or improve quality of life in substantial numbers of people, including economic impact where relevant.

B.2.4 For retrospective studies (e.g., chart reviews, secondary use of data and/or samples from a biobank or registry), background covers the population, condition, disease, intervention and/or outcomes studied, a discussion of standard of care where relevant, and the main research question(s), objective(s), or aim(s) (UHN REB CAPCR Presubmission Toolkit).

---

## B.3 Trial objectives and purpose

A description of the primary and secondary scientific objectives of the study and its purpose, including estimands when defined. Typically expressed as:
 - Statement of purpose (e.g., to assess, to determine, to compare, to evaluate)
 - General purpose (e.g., efficacy, safety, immunogenicity, pharmacokinetics)
 - Specific purpose (e.g., dose-response, superiority to placebo)
 - Name(s) of the intervention(s) being evaluated, with doses/dose ranges and dose regimens

---

## B.4 Trial design

A description of the trial design, covering:

B.4.1 The primary and secondary endpoints, if any, to be measured during the trial.

B.4.2 The type and design of trial (e.g., double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel design, adaptive design, platform/umbrella/basket, decentralised elements, observational or interventional, epidemiological design, open-label, dose-escalation), a schematic diagram of trial design/procedures/stages, the trial phase (I/II/III/IV), and whether it is single- or multicentre.

B.4.3 The measures taken to minimise/avoid bias: Randomisation or Blinding.

B.4.4 The investigational product(s) and their dosage/dosage regimen, including dosage form, packaging, and labelling.

B.4.5 Preparation (e.g., reconstitution) and administration instructions, where applicable.

B.4.6 The schedule of events (trial visits, interventions, assessments), including what data is collected at each time point and by what method — commonly accompanied by a subject visit schedule flow chart or timeline covering recruitment, consent, randomization, intervention, and follow-up.

B.4.7 The expected duration of participant involvement and the sequence/duration of all trial periods including follow-up, typically expressed as a month-by-month timeline.

B.4.8 The "stopping rules"/"discontinuation criteria" and "dose adjustment"/"dose interruption" criteria, for individual participants, parts of the trial, or the entire trial.

B.4.9 Accountability procedures for the investigational product(s), placebo(s), and other comparator(s), including the rationale for the comparison group chosen (e.g., active control/placebo).

B.4.10 Maintenance of treatment randomisation codes and procedures for breaking codes. Additional design elements commonly covered: the number of study groups/arms and their sample sizes; any stratification; centralization of evaluations (e.g., a central laboratory or central reading centre); and interim analysis plans.

B.4.11 The randomization allocation ratio (e.g., 1:1) alongside the number of study cohorts; for qualitative or mixed-methods designs, the data collection method(s) used (e.g., focus groups, interviews); additional or optional study components (e.g., videorecording, pharmacokinetic (PK) testing, genetic research); and, for multi-site studies, which activities occur at each site.

B.4.12 Standard of care: the current standard of care (SoC) for the study population at the site (e.g., relevant medication, intervention, clinical management) and how the study differs from or adds to it (e.g., different intervention, additional procedures, extra visits, changes in routine treatment), including whether differences occur during routine care visits or require additional time. Also covers medications/treatments commonly used for the condition that may interact with the study intervention, and whether the study intervention is itself standard of care.

---

## B.5 Selection of participants

The target sample size (actual numbers to be enrolled), the source of the study population (e.g., inpatient hospital setting, outpatient clinics, student health service, general public — including site names for single-center studies), and the recruitment method (e.g., advertisements, notices).

B.5.1 Participant inclusion criteria — typically covering:
 - Age and sex range
 - Willingness/ability to comply with study procedures for the study duration
 - General health status, diagnosis of a specific condition/disease, or specific clinical signs/symptoms/findings
 - Laboratory results within or outside a specific range
 - Contraception requirements for participants of reproductive potential (ICH M3)

B.5.2 Participant exclusion criteria — typically covering:
 - A medical condition, laboratory finding, or physical exam finding that precludes participation
 - Disallowed concomitant medications
 - Presence of specific devices (e.g., orthodontic appliances, dentures)
 - Recent febrile illness
 - Pregnancy or lactation
 - Known allergic reactions to the study product(s)
 - Recent treatment with another investigational drug or intervention
 - History of drug/alcohol abuse
 - Investigator judgment that participation would pose increased risk or preclude compliance/completion

B.5.3 Mechanism for pre-screening, where appropriate, and screening of participants.

B.5.4 Recruitment process — where participants are recruited from (e.g., a specific clinic, community advertisements, an existing registry/database), who identifies or screens potential participants (e.g., treating physician/clinical care team, study coordinator, registry staff), and how initial contact occurs (e.g., during a routine visit, phone call, letter, poster, social media, or a secure Epic MyChart message). Also covers the recruitment materials used (e.g., posters/flyers, email invitations, website postings, social media advertisements) and, where medical records/Epic/registries are used to identify participants, the process for obtaining permission to contact them (e.g., a treating physician's initial inquiry, or Epic's "Ok to Contact" indicator, which permits contact for research but is not itself study consent).

---

## B.6 Discontinuation of trial intervention and participant withdrawal from trial

A list of reasons subjects may be discontinued from the study, including distinct criteria for subjects vs. cohorts where relevant, and the data collection/follow-up for withdrawn subjects. Specifies:
- When and how to discontinue participants from the trial/investigational product treatment
- The type and timing of the data to be collected for withdrawn/discontinued participants, and how it is handled
- Whether and how participants are replaced
- The follow-up for participants who have discontinued the investigational product

---

## B.7 Treatment and interventions for participants

The methods of assigning subjects to study groups/treatments, including randomization procedures where applicable — typically described or tabulated at a level (e.g., allocation ratio) that does not compromise randomization integrity (e.g., without revealing block sizes).

B.7.1 The treatment(s) administered — product name(s), dose(s), dosing schedule(s), dose-adjustment criteria, route/mode of administration, and treatment period(s) (including follow-up) for each treatment group/arm.

B.7.2 Medication(s)/treatment(s) permitted (including concomitant and rescue medication) and not permitted before and/or during the trial.

B.7.3 Strategies to monitor participant adherence to treatment.

B.7.4 Randomization procedures — whether the trial is randomized, the plan for maintaining randomization codes, and the timing/procedures for planned and unplanned code-breaking.

---

## B.8 Assessment of efficacy

The assessment of trial results: positive results (e.g., reduced incidence or severity of disease, cost of health services, other appropriate outcomes) and negative results (severity and frequency of side effects and complications).

B.8.1 Specification of the efficacy parameters, where applicable; for retrospective studies, the key outcomes or variables analyzed (UHN REB CAPCR Presubmission Toolkit).

B.8.2 Methods and timing for assessing, recording, and analysing efficacy parameters, including the procedures, timing, and activities of any trial-related committees (e.g., IDMC, adjudication committees) involved in assessing efficacy data.

---

## B.9 Assessment of safety

B.9.1 Specification of safety parameters.

B.9.2 The methods, extent, and timing for recording and assessing safety parameters, including the role of any trial-related committees (e.g., IDMC).

B.9.3 Procedures for obtaining reports of, recording, and reporting adverse events, including definitions of Adverse Event (AE) and Serious Adverse Event (SAE) (ICH E6), the individual(s) responsible for each step (e.g., Investigator, Sponsor, Monitor), determination of relatedness and severity, report distribution, required follow-up, and specific procedures for unexpected severe AEs/SAEs and pregnancies.

B.9.4 The type and duration of the follow-up of participants after adverse events and other events such as pregnancies.

B.9.5 The Data Safety Monitoring Plan (DSMP) for the study, covering the plans in place to protect subject safety and well-being and the integrity of collected data.

---

## B.10 Statistical considerations

B.10.1 The statistical methods employed, including timing/purpose of any planned interim analyses, the statistical criteria for stopping the trial, and who carries out the analysis.

B.10.2 The number of participants planned to be enrolled and the reason for the sample size — including the method, any tables/statistical software used for the calculation, and the power/clinical justification.

B.10.3 The level of significance used, or the threshold for success on the posterior probability in a Bayesian design.

B.10.4 The participants included in the planned analyses, the statistical methods used, procedures for handling intercurrent events and missing/unused/spurious data (aligned with the target estimands, per ICH E9(R1)), and how the primary and secondary outcomes are analyzed.

B.10.5 A statement that any deviation(s) from the statistical analysis plan will be described and justified in the clinical trial report.

---

## B.11 Direct access to source records

A statement, in the protocol or a separate documented agreement, that the investigator(s)/institution(s)/service provider(s) will permit trial-related monitoring, audits, regulatory inspections, and REB/IEC review, providing direct access to source records.

Identifies who has access to records — each site permits authorized representatives of the sponsor(s) and regulatory agencies to examine (and, when required by law, copy) clinical records for quality assurance reviews, audits, and evaluation of study safety and progress.

A description of source data (original records of clinical findings, observations, and other trial-relevant activities), including whether CRFs are used as source documents and which data are collected on CRFs versus from other sources.

---

## B.12 Quality control and quality assurance

B.12.1 Identified critical-to-quality factors, associated risks, and risk mitigation strategies, unless documented elsewhere.

B.12.2 The monitoring approaches that are part of the quality control process, including GCP compliance, clinical monitor follow-up, DSMB oversight, and data management.

B.12.3 The process for handling noncompliance with the protocol or GCP, how data are evaluated for protocol compliance and accuracy against source documents, the documents reviewed (e.g., CRFs, clinic notes, product accountability) with responsible parties and review frequency (per a quality management plan or site SOPs), and staff training methods and tracking.

---

## B.13 Ethics

Description of ethical considerations relating to the trial.

B.13.1 Ethical Standard: the ethical considerations, context, and guiding ethical principles for the conduct of the trial.

B.13.2 Research Ethics Board: the protocol, informed consent documents, and recruitment material provided to each site's IRB/REB for review and approval, including amendments.

B.13.3 Informed Consent Process: the procedures for obtaining and documenting informed consent, provisions for special populations (e.g., non-English speakers, children, illiterate or non-writing individuals, vulnerable populations), and the different consent forms needed for the study (e.g., screening, study participation, future use of specimens), kept separate from the protocol document.

B.13.4 Study Discontinuation: the plan should the study be discontinued, including continuation of therapy for subjects where appropriate and crossover to study drug for placebo recipients at study completion.

---

## B.14 Data handling and record keeping

Data handling/record-keeping procedures for maintaining subject confidentiality, data security requirements, and record retention per the sponsor's requirements, including the steps taken to ensure data are accurate, consistent, complete, and reliable (per ICH E6) — covering source documentation, CRFs, form-completion instructions, data handling procedures, and data monitoring.

B.14.1 Specification of data to be collected and the method of collection.

B.14.2 Identification of data recorded directly into data acquisition tools (no prior written/electronic record) and considered source records.

B.14.3 A statement that records will be retained in accordance with applicable regulatory requirements.

B.14.4 For retrospective studies, the data sources (e.g., patient charts, registries, databases, biobanks) and, where applicable, biobank sample processing/analysis and combination with external datasets or biobanks (UHN REB CAPCR Presubmission Toolkit).

---

## B.15 Financing and insurance

Details of funding bodies and budget, including direct and indirect costs, if not addressed in a separate agreement.

## B.16 Publication policy

Publication and authorship policies, if not addressed in a separate agreement.

## B.17 References and appendix

Referenced publications and supplementary matrials.
